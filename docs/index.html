Built around how Delaware eats

Star ratings won't tell you who still fries scrapple for breakfast, or which brewery pours its own beer with dinner. Delaware Eats is organized the way people here eat: scrapple, Wilmington italian, farm creameries and brewpubs, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. Every listed restaurant the state inspected shows the Division of Public Health's own record, never a grade from us, and Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on Route 1 in August.

What stays on your phone

Delaware Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Delaware Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The scrapple, Wilmington Italian, creamery, brewpub and other food lists are our own research from fall 2026: a page that names each place for the food (the Cape Gazette, Delaware's or Greater Wilmington's tourism office, or the place's own site), and something from 2025 or 2026 that shows it's open. The wider directory of 2,879 restaurants starts from Overture Maps' open place data, keeps the kinds of listing that proved reliable against the Division of Public Health's statewide list of inspected establishments, and adds inspected restaurants from that list.

Where can I get scrapple?

Our scrapple list has 7 hand-checked places, from a Smyrna sausage house to Wilmington and Newark brunch menus. In the web search, the scrapple list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check a Delaware restaurant's inspection?

Delaware has no restaurant grade, score or placard. The Division of Public Health inspects every restaurant in the state and publishes each inspection of the past two years: the date, the type and the Food Code sections cited. For each place, the app and this site show the latest routine inspection with the number of sections cited, any later follow-up, and the full list. We compute no grade.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
